# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

HomeOps is for generic households that want a free, understandable way to manage chores, home maintenance, errands, recurring responsibilities, and eventually larger household projects. The primary users are everyday household members, including people who are not technically savvy, who need to set up a household, invite others, assign or share work, and understand what needs attention without learning a complex productivity system first.

The product should also support a public marketing landing page that explains the value before login, then an authenticated app experience where household members do the work.

## Product Purpose

HomeOps provides a free household operating system for recurring chores, maintenance, errands, and shared responsibilities. It should help households see what needs doing, decide what is ready or blocked, keep recurring upkeep from being forgotten, and coordinate work across invited members.

Projects are a future premium feature: a project groups tasks toward a particular outcome, such as a lawn renovation, move, room refresh, repair effort, or contractor-heavy home improvement plan.

Success means a household can start simple, trust the app quickly, and grow into more structured planning only when the household work actually needs it.

## Positioning

HomeOps gives any household a free, approachable way to turn chores and maintenance into a clear shared operating system, with premium project planning available when work becomes outcome-driven.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, optimization-minded, and empathetic. The product should feel helpful without becoming bossy, organized without feeling corporate, and capable without asking users to think like project managers.

## Anti-references

HomeOps should not feel like an over-optimized enterprise dashboard, a technical admin console, or a power-user productivity tool that hides basic actions behind dense configuration. It should avoid UX that prizes theoretical efficiency over clarity for ordinary household members.

## Design Principles

Start understandable, then reveal power. The first experience should make the next household action obvious; advanced planning should appear when the task calls for it.

Optimize for real homes, not perfect workflows. The interface should tolerate interrupted work, changing priorities, shared responsibility, and tasks that depend on weather, money, time, or other people.

Plain language beats system language. Labels, empty states, and errors should use household words before architecture words.

Shared context reduces coordination cost. Status, due windows, blockers, ownership, and activity should help people understand why something matters and who can move it forward.

Free core value comes first. Premium project planning should feel like a natural extension for outcome-based efforts, not a gate around everyday household usefulness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. The product should use responsive layouts, keyboard-accessible workflows, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, readable contrast, and plain-language copy. Patterns should work for non-technical household members and for users coordinating work across different devices and levels of comfort with software.
